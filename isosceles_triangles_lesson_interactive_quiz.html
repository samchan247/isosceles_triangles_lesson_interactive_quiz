Anatomy of an Isosceles Triangle

An isosceles triangle has two sides of equal length, creating symmetry between its sides and interior angles.

  • Equal Sides Legs AB = AC (marked with matching tick marks)
  • Vertex A Top vertex formed by the two equal sides; Vertex angle a
  • Base BC The third, unequal side opposite Vertex A
  • Base Angles Angles adjacent to the base are equal: ∠ABC = ∠ACB = b

Worked Example: Finding Angles

In an isosceles triangle with equal sides AB = AC, knowing one angle lets you find the remaining two:

Step 1: Equal Base Angles Angles opposite the equal sides are equal:
∠ABC = ∠ACB = 70°
Step 2: Interior Angle Sum Theorem The sum of interior angles in any triangle is 180°:
70° + 70° + ∠BAC = 180°
Step 3: Solve for Vertex Angle ∠BAC = 180° − (70° + 70°) = 40°

Formal Proof of the Theorem

Theorem: In ΔABC, if AB = AC, then ∠B = ∠C.

Construction (Axis of Symmetry): Draw the angle bisector AD of ∠BAC, meeting base BC at D, so that ∠BAD = ∠CAD.
Proof: In ΔABD and ΔACD:
Statement Reason
1. AB = AC Given
2. ∠BAD = ∠CAD By construction
3. AD = AD Common side
4. ΔABD ≅ ΔACD SAS
5. ∴ ∠B = ∠C corr. ∠s, ≅ Δs

Property of an Isosceles Triangle

If two sides of a triangle are equal, then the angles opposite those sides are equal.

If AB = AC, then ∠B = ∠C

Geometric Proof Abbreviation base ∠s, isos. Δ
